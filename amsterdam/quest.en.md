# ❌❌❌ Conquer Amsterdam

**Ships, windmills, ferries, secret courtyards and Sinterklaas**

_Campaign from November 2026 · 12 zones · 1–2 zones per outing_

Amsterdam stands on thousands of wooden poles, and its coat of arms has three crosses: XXX. This is our city, but do you know its secrets? Where does a giant carry the whole world on his shoulders? Where is the bridge of three herrings? Where is the house as wide as a door? Conquer the zones in any order, collect coins and become the Admiral of Amsterdam!

![Map of Amsterdam](quest-map.png)

## 📜 How to play

1. We are one team against Amsterdam. 12 zones in any order, 1–2 per outing.
2. Challenge done? Tick the box ☐ and colour in the coins 🪙. You lead everyone to all the stops with the map and no hints? Another 🧭 +2.
3. Reached 🏁? The zone is CONQUERED! A sticker on the card and on its number on the big map.
4. 👑 Boss: a big challenge. It may not work the first time, so try again! ⚔️ Duel: me against the parents. I get the coins for playing either way; the last one (+1) is only for winning. ⏱ Against the clock. 📏 Measures are on the hunt card.
5. Coins add up over all the outings. Spend them whenever you like: your rank counts everything you have earned!

### 🛒 What coins buy

- 🪙×5 — A hint for a riddle
- 🪙×12 — I choose the snack
- 🪙×20 — A parent does a curse of my choice
- 🪙×35 — Ice cream or a sweet
- 🪙×60 — A small souvenir
- 🪙×120 — A big surprise from the parents

### 🌀 City curses

A curse falls when we enter a new zone and the die shows 1, or when we skip a challenge. Roll the die again: the number is the curse. It hits the whole team until we finish the next challenge.

1. We talk like pirates: "Arr!" at the end of every sentence
2. We walk like seagulls, arms as wings, until the next bridge
3. See a boat? Shout "Full steam ahead!"
4. One hand behind your back
5. The next challenge on one leg (where you can)
6. We ring like a tram: "Ding-ding!" at every corner

### 🎖️ Rank

Your rank counts ALL the coins you have earned. Spending coins does not take it away:

- **Deckhand** — under 30 coins
- **Sailor** — 🪙 30+
- **Bosun** — 🪙 65+
- **Navigator** — 🪙 105+
- **Captain** — 🪙 150+
- **Admiral** — 🪙 195+ and all 12 zones conquered

### 🔑 Answers and hints

#### 🏝️ 1. Western Islands

_The three man-made islands (Bickers-, Prinsen-, Realeneiland) were built up in 1610–1615 as an extension of the port: shipyards, tar works, herring smokehouses, warehouses. At first they were called the Front, Middle and Rear islands, later named after Jan Bicker, the Princes of Orange and the Reael family. In the late 19th century ships grew too big, the islands emptied, and after the war artists rediscovered them._

- Centraal → Zandhoek → Drieharingenbrug → Prinseneiland → Galgenstraat → Bickersgracht → Centraal. ~4 km, 1.5–2 h
- All outdoors; farm Tue–Sun 9–17 (no staff needed for charades)
- Free
- Cafés on Haarlemmerdijk
- **1.1 Golden Coin:** [Zandhoek](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.38769,4.88993) — ✔ Zandhoek 14, the "De Gouden Reael" stone: a real coin with Emperor Charles V.
  The real was a Spanish-Portuguese silver coin used everywhere in the early 17th century; the Charles V version showed the emperor's bust. The coin hung as a house sign on the home of Laurens Reael (later Governor-General of the East Indies), and the family took its surname from it. Around 1620 Reynier Reael bought up plots on the Rear Island, and around 1624 the island was named after him. In 1648 the family built a warehouse here (a herring packing house, per Dutch Wikipedia) with the same coin on its stone. Around 1800 an inn called "De Gouden Reael" opened in the building, and the whole neighbourhood took its name. Today it is a restaurant. For the kid: "The coin named a family, the family named an island, and the house named a whole neighbourhood!"
- **1.2 Captains' Row:** [Zandhoek, row of houses](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.38745,4.8901) — ✔ 13 houses, nos. 2–14. Sum: steps for one house × 13 (= 10 times + 3 times). Compare with the walk.
  Reynier Reael built 13 identical "captains' houses" (nos. 2–14) around 1645–46; the monuments register dates nos. 2, 3 and 5 to 1657. Sources don't say which captains rented them: these aren't merchants' mansions like on the canal ring but a modest row of rental houses. Basements and upper floors were let separately, and some still have split doors so you can chat with the neighbour over the top half. Until 1832 the houses stood right on the open water of the IJ; then the Westerdoksdijk cut them off from the river. Sand for raising the streets was unloaded here, hence Zandhoek, "sand corner". In 1943 the houses were protected so a tobacco factory couldn't demolish them. For the kid: "From these windows you used to see a sea of ships!"
- **1.3 Shipyard Birds:** [Prinseneiland](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.38615,4.88624) — ✔ Check the photo yourselves. A coot is black with a white beak and a white "bald" patch; herons often stand on boats and posts. No luck? Try at the farm (ducks, geese).
  Prinseneiland was sold in plots from 1623, mostly for timber and tar warehouses; probably named after the first Princes of Orange. There were "over 60" or "over 100" warehouses here (sources disagree) out of the city's ~900 brick warehouses. The Western Islands warehouses stored herring, grain, tobacco, wine, salt, tar, pitch and, yes, cat skins. They have spout gables, hoisting beams with hooks under the roof and hatch shutters; the shutters often show the goods or a city they traded with. Under a shelter by Galgenstraat there is still a winch that lifted tar barrels from boats. Since the 1950s artists and ordinary residents live in the warehouses. Words are still visible on the shutters, the goods or a trading city (e.g. "Schelvis", haddock). For the kid: "The word on the shutters is what was stored here 300 years ago!"
- **1.4 ⏱ Playground Minute:** [Playground, Bickersgracht](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.38563,4.88869) — ✔ Self-check: a parent counts the touches out loud. Didn't make it? Second try.
  A small playground on Bickerseiland (unnamed on OSM). For the kid: "Where you're running, people tarred boats 400 years ago!"
- **1.5 Farm Charades:** [Kinderboerderij De Dierencapel, Bickersgracht 207](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.38597,4.88874) — ✔ No staff needed: look into the yard (sheep, goats, pigs, rabbits, guinea pigs, ducks, geese, chickens). Fewer than 3 guessed? A new set of five. Farm open Tue–Sun 9–17, closed Mon.
  A children's farm by the water on Bickerseiland; it sells eggs and drinks. The island itself was bought in 1631 by the regent Jan Bicker: about 10 shipyards and a mansion with a tower from which he watched his ships being built. For the kid: "Where the goats are now, they used to build ships!"
- **👑 Bridge 320:** [Drieharingenbrug](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.38687,4.88727) — ✔ Drieharingenbrug, the "Three Herrings Bridge". The bridge is narrow and bikes use it: start when no bikes are coming; a parent walks behind and says "stop" if needed. Usually takes 2–3 tries. In 2024 a temporary bridge stood here (replacement), so check which one is there now; if the number isn't visible, count it by the name.
  A double drawbridge over the Realengracht between Realeneiland and Prinseneiland. In 1625 there was a lift bridge with nine spans here; in 1737 it was nicknamed the "Flying" bridge, probably because it opened quickly; in 1859 it became a pontoon bridge, and today's narrow foot-and-bike bridge dates from 1983. The name comes from a stone with three crowned herrings on the corner house Vierwindendwarsstraat 1; around 1676 the distillery "De Drie Gecroonde Haringen" worked next door. Amsterdam's bridge numbers aren't in order: a bridge that disappears takes its number with it. For the kid: "Find the three crowned herrings on the house by the bridge!"

#### 🌷 2. Jordaan and Canals

_The Brouwersgracht ("brewers' canal", since 1594) separates the Jordaan, the craftsmen's district, from the wealthy 17th-century canal ring with the Keizersgracht and Westerkerk. In 2007 readers of Het Parool voted Brouwersgracht the most beautiful street in the city. The three brother houses at Bloemgracht 87–91 (townsman, farmer and sailor on their stones) are 3 minutes from Westerkerk if you have the energy. On the way to Brouwersgracht is the West-Indisch Huis on Herenmarkt: here the West India Company decided to found New Amsterdam, the future New York; in the courtyard is a statue of Peter Stuyvesant with his wooden leg (the gate is often closed at weekends)._

- Centraal (Damrak piers) → Herenmarkt (West-Indisch Huis) → Brouwersgracht → Noordermarkt (Noorderkerk, Winkel 43) → Keizersgracht 123 → along Prinsengracht past the Anne Frank House → Westerkerk. ~2.5 km, 2 h
- All outdoors except Winkel 43 (daily, Sun from ~9–10). On Saturdays the Noordermarkt market (9–16) is also on the square: a bonus, not required
- Pie ~5–7 €
- Winkel 43 (queue on Saturdays because of the market, takeaway possible)
- Winkel 43
- **2.1 Photo Hunt: Three Fleets:** [Damrak / Prins Hendrikkade piers](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.3773,4.8968) — ✔ Boats: most at Damrak and Prins Hendrikkade: Stromma (Damrak piers), Lovers (orange, Prins Hendrikkade); also Flagship, Blue Boat, small open Those Dam Boat Guys boats (by Westerkerk). Any name on the side counts. Places in route order: 1) Posthoornkerk, Haarlemmerstraat 124. 2) West-Indisch Huis, Herenmarkt / Haarlemmerstraat 75. 3) Woutertje Pieterse and Femke, by Noordermarkt / Westerstraat (near 2.3). 4) Homomonument, by Westerkerk. 5) Anne Frank Huis, Prinsengracht 263, next to Westerkerk, before the boss. No timer: done if all photos are in before the boss.
  The 17th-century canal ring (Herengracht, Keizersgracht, Prinsengracht and Singel) was added to the UNESCO list in 2010; the main tour-boat piers are by the station. For the kid: "The canals used to carry goods to the warehouses, and now they carry people who look at those warehouses!" 1) Posthoornkerk (1860–63) is a Catholic church by Pierre Cuypers, who also built the station and the Rijksmuseum; its two towers rise high above Haarlemmerstraat. The name comes from an old "hidden church" in a house called the Post Horn (Posthoorn). 2) West-Indisch Huis (1617) was first a meat market, then the headquarters of the West India Company (WIC), which traded with the Americas: here in 1625 it was decided to build New Amsterdam, the future New York. In the courtyard stands Peter Stuyvesant, its governor with the wooden leg. 3) Woutertje Pieterse is the daydreaming boy from Multatuli's book, Femke is his friend; nearby is a statue of Theo Thijssen, teacher and writer from this neighbourhood. For the kid: "The boy from the book was always daydreaming. What do you dream about?" 4) Homomonument (1987): three pink granite triangles, one stepping down into the water, in memory of people persecuted for who they love; the first monument of its kind in the world. 5) Anne Frank Huis: here the girl Anne Frank and her family hid from the Nazis for two years in the back house behind a bookcase, and she kept a diary; from the window she heard the Westerkerk bells. For the kid, simply: "A girl hid here with her family and wrote a diary that people all over the world read now."
- **2.2 Corner Warehouses:** [Brouwersgracht 200–207](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.38161,4.88702) — ✔ No. 200 Vijfhoek ("five corners"), no. 205 Vierhoek ("four corners"), no. 207 Driehoek ("three corners"); the island is no. 204 Spitsbergen.
  The canal is named after its 16th–17th-century breweries, though by 1664 only 3 of the city's 22 breweries stood on it. The warehouses have their own names because houses had no numbers then: nos. 188–196 De Appel, Grouwe Valk, Groene Valk, De David, Koning David; no. 199 De Liefde; no. 206 "Little Green Deer", no. 210 "Big Green Deer". According to tourist sources they stored coffee, spices and leather (unconfirmed). Almost all are flats now. For the kid: "Warehouses are like dogs: each one has its own name!"
- **2.3 ⏱ Cross Church:** [Noorderkerk, Noordermarkt](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.37964,4.88643) — ✔ There are little houses in all four corners of the cross (check on the spot). Run on the Noordermarkt square and pavement, don't cross the road; on Saturdays and Mondays there's a market here, so walk then.
  Noorderkerk (1620–1623, Hendrick de Keyser) is Amsterdam's first church in the shape of a Greek cross: the pulpit is in the middle, so every worshipper, the poor people of the Jordaan, could hear well. The little houses in the corners were rented out and the money went to the church. For the kid: "The church is a plus sign, and people live in its corners!"
- **2.4 Dutch Pie:** [Winkel 43, Noordermarkt 43](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.37908,4.88623) — ✔ Order: "Een appeltaart met slagroom, alstublieft". Question: "Hoeveel appeltaarten bakken jullie per dag?" Per the Michelin Guide, up to 140. A slice ~5–7 €. Open daily, Sun from ~9–10. Queue on Saturdays because of the market.
  Per the Michelin Guide, Winkel 43's five bakers peel apples every day and bake up to 140 pies. Queues are worst on market days: the Saturday farmers' market and the Monday flea market on Noordermarkt. A Dutch apple pie recipe already appears in a cookbook from 1514 (unconfirmed). For the kid: "The Dutch have been baking this pie for over 500 years!"
- **2.5 House of Heads:** [Huis met de Hoofden, Keizersgracht 123](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.37634,4.88745) — ✔ 6 heads of Roman gods: laurels, Apollo; wheat, Ceres; grapes, Bacchus; crescent, Diana; helmets, Minerva and Mars (other sources: Mercury in a winged helmet). They are gods, not thieves.
  This 1622 double house was built for the merchant Nicolaes Sohier; the facade is attributed to Hendrick de Keyser. The legend (from the 17th century): six burglars climbed into the house and a deaf cook chopped them down one by one with an axe; as a reward the owner put their heads on the facade, and the seventh, "missing" head is supposedly the maid herself. In a 1911 version there were seven thieves. As early as 1861 the story was called made up. In fact they are busts of six Roman gods, about 1 m tall: Apollo (laurels), Ceres (wheat), Mercury (winged helmet), Minerva (plumed helmet), Bacchus (grapes), Diana (crescent). Mercury (trade) and Minerva (wisdom) flank the entrance: the owner, arms king Louis de Geer, wanted to show himself as a "wise merchant". From 1656 to 1670 the educator Jan Amos Comenius lived here; today it is the Embassy of the Free Mind museum. For the kid: "Find each head's sign!"
- **👑 Tower Crown:** [Westerkerk](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.37455,4.88399) — ✔ The crown is blue (gold from 1906 to 2006). Best photo from Westermarkt, south of the tower. Strikes on the hour (1–12). The Homomonument is next to it: it's quiet there, and we don't stand in the Anne Frank House queue.
  Westerkerk was built in 1620–1631 to a design by Hendrick de Keyser; its 87 m tower is the tallest church tower in the city, and the carillon has 51 bells. Rembrandt was buried here in 1669 as a poor man, nobody knows exactly where; Anne Frank mentions this tower's clock in her diary. Why a crown: in the 1480s Amsterdam lent money to Maximilian of Habsburg and in return got the right to a crown in its coat of arms (in 1497 the city even paid to have it confirmed). The crown on the tower (1638) copies the crown of Emperor Rudolf II. It was gilded for Rembrandt's 300th birthday in 1906 and turned blue again in 2006. For the kid: "The city got its crown for lending the emperor money!" Anne Frank, in the hiding place at Prinsengracht 263, two minutes from here, heard these bells and wrote about them in her diary.

- **11.1 King's Gate:** [Haarlemmerpoort](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.38497,4.88308) — ✔ 8 columns (check the split per side on site). The gate opened on 27.11.1840. Count from the pavement: bikes ride through the arch, don't stand in the passage.
  Its official name is Willemspoort, but nobody calls it that. It is the fifth gate on the road to Haarlem; the previous one, by de Keyser (1615), was demolished in 1837. The new one opened on 27 November 1840: Willem II rode through it the day before he took the throne. The passage has 8 columns of Bentheim sandstone. Tax on incoming goods was collected here, later it housed police and firemen; in 1889 the city decided to demolish it but changed its mind. Since 1986 it has held flats. For the kid: "A king rode in through this gate!"
- **11.2 Saw Mill:** [De Otter, Gillis van Ledenberchstraat 78](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.37609,4.87119) — ✔ A paltrok: the whole body sits on a ring of rollers and is turned with a winch at the back (check on site). If it isn't working, look from outside; the sails may be missing.
  A wooden "paltrok" sawmill: it stands on a short post and a ring of rollers and turns to the wind all in one piece. Inside, three vertical saws cut logs into planks. Of a whole group of sawmills along the Kostverlorenvaart, only this one is left. The sails were removed in 1925; the mill was restored in 1994–96 (span 19.5 m), but between the houses it doesn't get enough wind. For the kid: "The wind sawed the logs!"
- **11.3 Giant's Measure:** [De 1200 Roe, Haarlemmerweg 701](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.38424,4.81512) — ✔ A roe ≈ 3.7 m (Amsterdam 3.68 m, Rhineland 3.77 m; sources differ). 5 adult steps of ~75 cm. A child ~1.25 m tall fits ~3 times. 1200 roe ≈ 4.5 km, about how far you came from the gate by bus. Do the caterpillar on the grass on the mill side, away from Haarlemmerweg (busy road).
  This octagonal "grondzeiler" mill (sails reaching almost to the ground) from 1632 drained the Osdorper Binnenpolder. The roe (roede, "rod") is an old measure of length that differed from city to city: Amsterdam's was 13 feet, 3.68 m, Rhineland's 3.77 m. In 1951 the polder was filled in for a housing estate, but the mill stayed; it works, but people live in it, so you can't go inside. For the kid: "The mill's name is a distance!"
- **11.4 I'm a Windmill!:** [Lawn by Molen van Sloten](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.3418,4.7925) — ✔ The lawn by Molen van Sloten. Big circles, straight arms: the parent checks the kid plays fair, and the kid checks the parent.
- **👑 Inside the Windmill:** [Molen van Sloten, Akersluis 10](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.3416,4.79227) — ✔ A tour every hour, included in the ticket. In the attic, figures from Rembrandt's paintings (he was a miller's son), according to reviews. "Hoe staan de wieken als er feest is?" Joy (vreugdestand): the top sail stops just short of the very top; mourning (rouwstand): just past the top (check with the miller). An Archimedes screw (vijzel) lifts the water from the polder into the Ringvaart. In winter 10–16, last tour 15:00.
  A polder mill: together with a pumping station it drains water from the western districts. It was assembled in 1991 on a new base from the body of an 1847 mill from Watergraafsmeer; the cap and sails are new (the sails were replaced in 2024). The attic has an exhibition on Rembrandt, the miller's son; next to the mill stand statues of Rembrandt and Saskia. For the kid: "This mill keeps our feet dry!"

#### 🌳 12. Amsterdamse Bos

_In the Great Depression Amsterdam had over 50,000 unemployed, and the city turned the "Boschplan" into a work project for them: from 30 April 1934 to 1940 ~20,000 people worked here, mostly with shovels and wheelbarrows. Today it is ~1000 ha and ~200,000 trees; the water inside lies ~5.5 m below sea level._

- Entrance by the Bosbaan → along the canal → goat farm → Big Meadow (wheelbarrow) → ball bridge no. 520 → Speeleilanden. ~3.5 km, 2.5–3 h
- The forest is always open
- Free

- **12.1 Old Tram:** [Haarlemmermeerstation](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.34934,4.85672) — ✔ The museum line runs on Sundays from April to October, 11–17 (check the 2027 season). The museum's trams come from Amsterdam, Vienna, Prague and other cities: the maker's plate is usually by the door or above a window.
  The museum line opened in 1975 on the last stretch of an old 1915 railway: 7.2 km. It runs trams from 1904–1960 from Amsterdam, The Hague, Rotterdam, Vienna and Prague. For the kid: "This tram is older than Grandma, and it might have come from Vienna!"
- **12.2 ⏱ Race the Boat:** [Bosbaan](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.32616,4.8364) — ✔ Measure 100 m with phone GPS. An eight: 2000 m in ~5.5–6 min → 100 m in ~17–18 s. Rowers often train on Sundays.
  The Bosbaan was dug first, from 1934, by hand by unemployed men; the first races were in 1937. It is 2200 m long; it was 72 m wide, now 118 m with 8 racing lanes. World rowing championships have been held here. For the kid: "This huge canal was dug with shovels!" The Bosbaan (1936–1937) was the world's first artificial rowing course of this length, 2200 m; the boathouse and the grandstand at the start are listed monuments.
- **12.3 Goat Farm:** [Geitenboerderij Ridammerhoeve, Nieuwe Meerlaan 4](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.31287,4.82469) — ✔ From 1.11 the farm is closed Mon and Tue, other days 10–17 (open Sunday). The café has goat's milk ice cream and coffee, the shop goat's cheese. There probably won't be kids in November.
  A biodynamic goat farm in the forest (the address is in Amstelveen). It started in 1987; back then it was the largest goat farm in the country, now with its ~120 goats it is one of the smallest. The milk becomes cheese, yoghurt and even ice cream. Chickens, pigs, cows, a horse and ponies live here too. For the kid: "120 goats, and ice cream from their milk!"
- **12.4 ⏱ Human Wheelbarrow:** [Grote Speelweide](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.32017,4.82715) — ✔ "De Kruiwagen" is a monument to the unemployed who built the forest by hand in the 1930s. Wet grass: bring wipes; the parent holds at the knees, not the feet.
  The Grote Speelweide is the forest's central meadow. The Wheelbarrow monument remembers the 20,000 unemployed men who built the forest by hand. For the kid: "Find the wheelbarrow: this forest was built with wheelbarrows!"
- **12.5 Weighted Bridges:** [Bridges around the Grote Speelweide](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.32031,4.82497) — ✔ The ball bridges are designed by Piet Kramer: no. 520 (1938) by the Big Meadow, one of a series of five by the Bosbaan; no. 549, the "red" one with 3 balls, at the east end of the Bosbaan; also no. 548 (6 balls) and no. 592. The balls are counterweights for the moving deck; the hinges are at one edge.
  The forest has 116 bridges; many were drawn by Piet Kramer, a master of the Amsterdam School who designed about 500 of the city's bridges. He mixed wood, brick, steel and concrete. The coloured balls are not decoration but part of the counterweight. For the kid: "The balls are weights that lift the bridge!"
- **👑 Forest Boat with a Passenger:** [Speeleilanden, Grote Vijver](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=52.3213,4.8268) — ✔ Sank, or the passenger fell off? Fix it and try again. Launch it holding a parent's hand. Don't break living branches, don't touch mushrooms, leave the boat in the forest.
  The Speeleilanden are play islands in the Big Pond (Grote Vijver): a ferry you pull yourself, rope bridges, rafts and a place for building huts, open all year and free. In the Amsterdamse Bos you may build with fallen branches, but you can't take anything away; people have even been fined here for picking mushrooms. For the kid: "People planted this whole forest, and you'll build the boat!"
